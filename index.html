What decides a taxi fare and a tip?

Six thousand New York taxi rides from one month, March 2019. A small dataset, but enough to show what this course asks of a data story: honest charts with active titles, a one-sentence takeaway under each, numbers in context, and a conclusion that takes a side.

Demo team: your names and student IDs go here · Data: NYC Taxi & Limousine Commission sample via seaborn-data · Data card

Research questions

  1. How are fares distributed, and how far does the expensive tail reach?
  2. How closely does distance explain the fare, and does the payment method matter?
  3. When do people ride, by day of the month and by hour, and where do they tip most?

Conclusion

Distance sets the fare and the card reader sets the tip size; what differs between boroughs is whether a tip is recorded at all. If you are a rider, the only lever you control is the trip length; if you are a fleet operator, the weekday evening peak is where the money is. The data do not support stories about generous or stingy neighbourhoods: outside Manhattan the sample is thin and the zero tips may be a recording artefact of green cabs as much as a behaviour.

Limitations and ethics

  • This is a 6,433-trip sample of one month, not the full record; absolute counts are not city totals.
  • Cash tips are not recorded, so tipping conclusions apply to card payments only.
  • Trips longer than 180 minutes and zero-fare rows were removed; see the data card for every cleaning decision.
  • The data contain no personal identifiers; zone-level locations are published by the City of New York.
